# Sprint 1 Retrospective

**Project:** Migrant Hub — student onboarding and blogging platform
**Sprint:** Sprint 1 (30 August – 3 September 2026)
**Team:** Pratham Arora, Yun Wang, Prabhleen Kaur, Sajib Das, Sehwinder Singh
**Attendance:** all five members present at every meeting

---

## What went well

- **We met daily except Sundays**, over Zoom and WhatsApp calls, with WhatsApp used for
  coordination between meetings. Attendance was full nobody missed a session.
- **Work was split by strength rather than divided equally.** Each member owned an area
  they were best placed to deliver: backlog, prototype, content, repository, presentation.
- **Ideas were shared openly.** Everyone contributed to the product discussion, not only
  to their own task.
- **Nobody stayed stuck.** Daily meetings plus continuous updates meant blockers surfaced
  and were picked up the same day.
- **Responsibilities were clear.** No member was unsure what they owned at any point in
  the sprint.

## What did not go well

- Deciding the product idea took a long time and then longer to reach a version that
  everyone was genuinely happy with rather than one people merely accepted.
- Because the idea was settled late, the rest of the sprint was compressed into a few
  days. We delivered, but not at a steady pace.
- We learned about some deliverables later than we should have, which meant work was
  discovered rather than planned.

## Decisions that came out of the sprint

The scope moved in both directions as we learned more:

- **Cut** the restaurant listing feature — Google Maps already does it better than we could.
  What we keep is students writing about the experience, which is the part Google does not have.
- **Added** HOAS housing guidance to the Must Do section — as a guide with official links,
  not a booking feature, because we cannot integrate with HOAS.
- **Redefined** a community as a group of posts rather than a social feature, to keep the
  scope achievable.
- **Deferred** community chat and events to a later version.

## Team development stage

**Assessed stage: late Storming, moving into Norming.**

Our friction was not interpersonal — it was disagreement about what to build, and it took
real time to resolve. Having resolved it, we produced the agreements a Norming team makes:
a working agreement, a Definition of Done, a branching and pull request policy, and a rule
for settling disagreements.

We are not Performing and would not claim to be after one sprint: the late decision
compressed the sprint, so we are not yet delivering predictably.

The Scrum Master role rotates for Sprint 2. Scrum does not require permanent ownership of a
role, and rotating it spreads the process knowledge rather than leaving it with one person.
